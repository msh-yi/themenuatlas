# The Menu Atlas (static site)

A static page that loads `data/data.json` and does search, filtering and charts in the browser. `export-site` splits the data so the first load stays light: `data.json` (lists, map, charts; compacted), `pairs.json` (loaded by the pairing and ingredient pages) and, with `--menus`, `menus/NN.json` (64 shards, loaded when a restaurant page opens; `NN` is FNV-1a of the slug, mod 64, the same in `site.py` and `app.js`). No build step and no dependencies.

```bash
uv run menudb export-site                  # synthetic fixture -> site/data/data.json
cd site && python3 -m http.server 8000     # open http://localhost:8000
```

- `menudb export-site --source bucket|db` writes real aggregated data. `site/data/` is gitignored; real data goes public only through the Pages deploy in `site.yml`, never through the repo (see "Website" in `docs/PLAN.md`).
- The data holds derived facts only: ingredient names, preparations, shares, pair scores and coverage, plus restaurant names and website links. No course text; per-course ingredient lists only with `--menus` (see **Menus** below).
- **Map** (`#/coverage`) is an ingredient atlas: on a world map (Equal Earth projection, drawn on a canvas), each region with at least 5 restaurants' menus is lettered with its signature ingredients, those on at least 4% of its menus at 1.6× or more their share everywhere, used by a few of its restaurants, ranked by share × log2(ratio). More appear as you zoom in; region blocks are nudged apart so they don't collide. "Where is it on the menu?" (`?i=<ingredient>`) shades the regions by that ingredient's share instead. Country outlines are Natural Earth 1:110m (public domain) in `world.js`, regenerated with `scripts/make_world.py`. The restaurant map now only appears on ingredient pages ("Where it's on the menu").
- **Flavour compass** (each ingredient page, under the numbers): its 24 strongest pairings around it. Each kind of ingredient present gets a slice of the circle sized by how many partners it has, in a fixed order; closer to the middle is a stronger pairing (score), a bigger dot more restaurants. Clicking a partner opens the dishes with both. It replaces the pairs table.
- **Seasons are switched off** on the site (the export still writes `by_season`), and ingredient pages no longer show "A kind of" / "Kinds": the lexicon's parent links mix kinds with things made from an ingredient, so they're only used to leave such pairs out of pairings.
- **Accolades** come from the `lists` column of the seed and full lists: Michelin stars from the most recent guide year listed, World's 50 Best rank, NYT America 50, and NYT NYC 100 rank. Restaurants and the coverage map filter and group by them. Ingredient shares and pairings by accolade are computed per group (`by_accolade`, `pairs_by_accolade`, at most 3,000 pairs each), only for groups with at least 10 restaurants with menus (`site.MIN_GROUP`).
- **Menus** (`export-site --menus` only): each restaurant page lists its menus course by course, with ingredient links and preparations, never the menu's own wording. The Pages build includes them.
- Views use hash routes (`#/` home, `#/ingredients`, `#/ingredient/<name>`, `#/pairings`, `#/restaurants`, `#/restaurant/<slug>`, `#/coverage` (the Map), `#/about`, and `#/courses?i=<ingredient>&prep=<preparation>`, `&as=<other name>` or `&with=<ingredient>`), and filters live in the URL so a view can be linked.
- **Home** (`#/`) is a landing page: one search box over ingredients (and their other names) and restaurants with menus, then cards for Ingredients, Restaurants, Pairings and the Map.
- **Restaurants without menus are hidden by default** everywhere: the restaurant list defaults to "With menus" (`menus=all` shows the whole list) and the map shows only "Menus collected" until other statuses are turned on in the legend (`hide=none` shows all).
- **Look:** the "Lush forest" greens (`#2E6F40`, `#CFFFDC`, `#68BA7F`, `#253D2C`) on warm ivory, with brass (`#A8762C`) as the contrasting accent: green for the header and links, brass for chart bars, rules and selected states. Tokens in `style.css`, with a dark mode. Headings use Cormorant Garamond and all other text (body, tables, forms) Newsreader, both from Google Fonts. Newsreader is a text serif drawn for on-screen reading, with optical sizes so small table text stays sturdy. Michelin stars are drawn with the Michelin symbol (`#mstar` in `index.html`, traced from Wikimedia Commons' `Etoile_Michelin-1.svg`).
- `.github/workflows/site.yml` builds it from the synthetic fixture (as an artifact) on pushes to `main` that touch the site, and from Postgres after each successful `check` run or such a push, deploying that build to GitHub Pages.
- **Pairings** leave out the obvious, by `data/lexicon/pairings.txt` (edit it, then rebuild): `generic` ingredients ("fish", "herbs", "stock") are never paired; `same` pairs (fig + fig leaf) and an ingredient with its own kind in the lexicon (mushroom > porcini) are dropped; `staple` ingredients (bread, butter, pasta, ...) are flagged `pairing: "staple"` and their pairings hidden until the viewer ticks "Include staples" (`staples=1` in the URL).
- **Website suggestions:** a restaurant page with no website, or no menu found, links to the issue form `website.yml`, prefilled with its slug. A maintainer checks the link and adds the `website-approved` label; `website-suggestion.yml` then sets the website (`website_source=suggested`), runs discovery on it, commits, and answers and closes the issue.
- **Opt-outs:** restaurants ask through the repo's issue form (linked from About, the footer and each restaurant page). Adding the slug to `data/seed/optouts.csv` removes it from the export entirely.
- **Pairings** show plain language only: the chart's axes read "stronger association" (lift, log scale) and "more popular" (support, log scale), and the table shows a strength bar (the shrunk-lift score) and restaurant count. Clicking a dot, a pair, or a "Pairs with" row on an ingredient page opens every dish with both (`#/courses?i=a&with=b`).
- **Course lists** (`#/courses`): the preparation and "Also on menus as" tags on an ingredient page open every course, across all restaurants, where it appears that way, each as its ingredients (loads all 64 menu shards). For "as", the export keeps the name a menu used for an ingredient only when the lexicon lists it as one of that ingredient's names, and counts restaurants per name (`aka_n`).
